# Methods summary: Evaluation of Land and Water Resource Management for Pantaron Range, Mindanao

## Evaluation logic

The study will compare selected complete catchments through three evidence layers:

1. **Resource condition:** land cover/forest configuration, terrain, riparian cover, erosion susceptibility, rainfall, drainage, surface-water occurrence, and targeted field observations.
2. **Pressures and risks:** verified land-cover conversion, forest edges, steep cultivated or exposed areas, riparian disturbance, rainfall variability, and supported reported pressures.
3. **Management response:** documented protection, restoration, sustainable-land-management, monitoring, water-management, and governance actions.

Remote sensing will evaluate resource condition and change. It cannot independently establish land tenure, actual management practice, water availability, water quality, or management effectiveness.

## Core procedure

1. Verify a Pantaron reference boundary and select two to four complete management catchments with documented outlets.
2. Audit all data for provider, version, period, native resolution, units, CRS, missing values, license, and retrieval date.
3. Map land cover from quality-screened Sentinel-2 Level-2A composites. Add change only if two periods are comparable and independently validated.
4. Calculate forest configuration, riparian cover, slope, and terrain constraints. Map erosion susceptibility; use RUSLE only if all factors and validation evidence are defensible.
5. Delineate drainage from Copernicus DEM GLO-30, test outlet and stream-threshold sensitivity, and compare with reference hydrography.
6. Characterize CHIRPS v3 rainfall totals, seasonality, anomalies, and dry spells at native 0.05-degree support.
7. Conduct targeted field validation for uncertain land classes, riparian condition, erosion indicators, and accessible streams. Calibrate instruments and document QA/QC.
8. Inventory management interventions from official and traceable records, distinguishing planned, implemented, monitored, and evaluated actions.
9. Classify management needs—protect, restore, apply sustainable land management, monitor, or investigate—using transparent rules, sensitivity tests, and evidence-strength ratings.

## Field methods boundary

Land-validation observations should record coordinates, date/time, dominant cover, photographs, observer confidence, and access limitations. Stream observations may include geometry, velocity/discharge, riparian condition, temperature, pH, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, and turbidity when calibrated equipment and protocols are available. A single visit is a snapshot, not evidence of dependable supply, long-term water quality, or compliance.

## Management effectiveness boundary

Spatial overlap between an intervention and a resource condition does not prove effectiveness. Effectiveness should only be assessed when intervention location, timing, implementation, outcome indicators, and a defensible comparison are available. Otherwise, report management coverage, implementation evidence, and monitoring gaps.
